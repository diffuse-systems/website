Why Diffuse Enterprise

The deployment that passes the audit

Most organisations already run a language model somewhere. Almost none of them could survive being asked to prove who used it, on what, and under whose authority.

The usual state

Scattered

A tool per team

A developer's inference server here, a notebook there, a container somebody started in March. Nothing knows about anything else, and no single question about the whole estate has an answer.

The consequence

Unprovable

No trail to produce

When the auditor asks who ran what against which weights on the fourteenth, the honest answer is that nobody recorded it, because nothing in that stack was built to.

The outcome

Failed

A finding, not a warning

For regulated and critical infrastructure this is not a remark in an annexe. It is the control that was not implemented, and the project stops until it is.

What goes wrong

The dev-tool deployment, examined

The tools that make a model easy to try are not the tools that make one defensible to run. Each of these is ordinary, and each is a finding.

Access

An open port is an open door

The usual inference server binds a port and serves whoever reaches it. Open it beyond the workstation and anyone on the network can send requests: no token, no account, no identity, no limit. The access control is the firewall rule, and firewall rules are edited by people in a hurry.

Transport

Plaintext across the estate

Prompts, documents and completions cross the network unencrypted because the default was http and nobody changed it. In an environment where transport encryption is a stated control, that single default is the difference between a deployment and an incident.

Identity

Nobody is anybody

There is no notion of who made a request. Not a certificate, not an account, not a key. Attribution is impossible after the fact, which means every question that begins "who" cannot be answered at all.

Audit

Nothing is written down

No record of who deployed which weights, who revoked what, who was refused and why. A log of crashes is not an audit trail, and reconstructing one from three teams' shell histories is not evidence.

Governance

No centre, so no control

Each team runs its own. Nobody can say how many models are running, on what hardware, under which licences, with which data. A control you cannot enumerate is a control you do not have.

Reach

Available to the few

Because it is somebody's machine under somebody's desk, it serves that team. The rest of the organisation either does without or starts a second one, and now there are two of everything to not audit.

Reasons for Diffuse

What we built instead

Every line above has a counterpart that was designed rather than retrofitted. This is the whole argument for this product.

Access

An identity, or nothing

Every connection between machines is mutually authenticated TLS 1.3. There is no plaintext transport in any configuration, including development defaults, and no flag that turns it off. A machine's identity comes from its certificate, never from what it claims in a request.

Applications

Keys that expire and narrow

Application access is an API key, hashed at rest, shown once, with an expiry and a scope. A reporting service gets a key that reaches one model and stops working on a date you chose.

Audit

Who did what, and what was refused

An append-only trail of every administrative action, with the actor resolved from their certificate or session. Refusals are recorded as deliberately as successes, because a trail that holds only what worked cannot answer whether somebody tried.

Confidentiality

Customer content never reaches it

Not prompts, not completions, not the rows of a training corpus, not a document's text. Asserted by tests that send a sentinel through a real request and then search every column and both service logs for it.

Governance

One centre that decides

One coordinator holds the identities, the entitlement, the placement and the trail. Enumerating what runs where is one command, and roles separate the people who deploy from the people who read the trail.

Reach

Available to the organisation

One endpoint, OpenAI-compatible, that every team points at. Existing clients work unchanged, and access is granted and withdrawn centrally rather than by who knows which port.

None of this makes an audit pass by itself. What it does is make the answers exist: the trail is there to produce, the identities are there to enumerate, and the transport was never in question. That is the difference between preparing for an audit and rebuilding for one.

Where it runs

Your hardware, wherever it is

On-premises is where this started and it is not the limit. What the product requires is that the machines are yours to control, not that they sit in your building.

Option one

The fleet you already own

Workstations and lab machines, idle after six. Capacity you already pay for becomes capacity that serves.

Option two

Your own cloud account

GPU instances in your account and your network. You pay for the machine rather than for tokens, and nothing is metered by anybody else.

Option three

One box on a desk

An AMD Ryzen AI Halo or an NVIDIA DGX Spark: 128 GB of unified memory in a compact, quiet case. One machine holds the model whole, for the whole company. Splitting is the fallback, never the requirement.
